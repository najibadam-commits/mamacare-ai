# Malaria in Pregnancy - Prevention and Warning Signs

## Why malaria is dangerous in pregnancy
Pregnant women are more likely to get malaria and to become seriously ill from it. Malaria in pregnancy can cause anaemia, miscarriage, stillbirth, early birth and a baby with low birth weight.

## How to prevent malaria: sleep under an insecticide-treated net
Sleep under an insecticide-treated mosquito net (ITN) every night, all year round, including in the dry season.
Hang the net so it is tucked in well around the bed. Check it for holes and repair them. Antenatal clinics often give free nets.
Clear standing water around the home and keep drains clean, because mosquitoes breed there. Screens on windows and doors also help.

## How to prevent malaria: preventive treatment given at the clinic (IPTp)
Intermittent preventive treatment in pregnancy (IPTp) is a malaria-prevention medicine given to every pregnant woman at the antenatal clinic, even if she feels well.
The World Health Organization recommends that IPTp starts from about thirteen weeks of pregnancy and is given at each scheduled antenatal visit, at least one month apart, until delivery.
In Nigeria the health worker gives it at the clinic and watches you take it. Do not buy malaria medicines from roadside sellers and do not take them on your own.
Going to every antenatal visit is how you receive each scheduled dose.

## Symptoms that need a health worker
Malaria can cause fever, chills and shivering, headache, body aches, vomiting, loss of appetite and feeling very weak.
Fever in pregnancy should never be ignored. Go to a health facility the same day for a malaria test and the right care.
Some medicines are not safe in pregnancy, so only use medicines given or approved by your midwife or doctor. Do not treat yourself.

## Severe malaria is an emergency
Go to a hospital immediately if there is a high fever with chills together with fits, confusion or unusual sleepiness, difficulty breathing, very severe weakness, yellow eyes, very pale skin, or repeated vomiting.
These can be signs of severe malaria or another serious illness. A midwife or doctor must see you without delay.

## Test and follow up
A rapid malaria test at the clinic gives a quick result. Tell the health worker you are pregnant, or may be pregnant, at every visit.
After treatment, return for follow-up if the health worker asks you to.
