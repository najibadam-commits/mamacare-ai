# Antenatal Nutrition and Supplements

## Why nutrition matters
Good food in pregnancy supports the baby's growth and the mother's strength. It also lowers the risk of anaemia, which is common among pregnant women in Nigeria.
Eat a variety of foods every day. Eat a little more as the pregnancy advances, especially in the second and third trimesters.

## Iron and folic acid
Health workers give daily iron and folic acid supplements at antenatal clinics. They help prevent anaemia and protect the baby's developing brain and spine.
Take the supplements exactly as your midwife or doctor explains. The health worker decides the right amount for you.
Folic acid is most useful early, ideally before conception or in the first twelve weeks of pregnancy.
Take iron tablets with water or a citrus fruit such as orange. Tea and coffee reduce how well the body absorbs iron, so keep them away from iron tablets and meals.
Dark stools or mild constipation can happen with iron. Tell your health worker if the tablets make you feel unwell, and do not stop them without asking.

## Iron-rich and healthy foods
- Beans, groundnuts, lentils and soya give protein and iron.
- Eggs, fish, chicken and lean meat, well cooked, give protein and iron.
- Dark green leafy vegetables such as ugu, spinach and moringa leaves help build healthy blood.
- Orange and yellow foods such as pawpaw, mango and carrots give vitamins for the mother and baby.
- Whole grains such as millet, maize, sorghum and brown rice give energy and fibre.
- Milk, pasteurised yoghurt and other dairy foods give calcium for the baby's bones.
- Drink plenty of clean, safe water through the day.

## Foods and habits to avoid
- Avoid raw or undercooked meat, fish and eggs.
- Avoid unpasteurised milk and soft cheese.
- Avoid alcohol and smoking at any time in pregnancy.
- Avoid herbal mixtures and traditional remedies unless your health worker has approved them.
- Avoid eating clay, chalk or other non-food items. Tell your health worker if you crave them.
- Wash vegetables and fruit well, and wash your hands before cooking and eating.
- Limit tea, coffee and sugary drinks.

## Small meals and nausea
Nausea is common in the first months. Eat small meals often, sip water between meals, and try dry biscuits or pap in the morning before getting out of bed.
Avoid very oily or spicy foods if they upset you.

## Antenatal visits
The World Health Organization recommends at least eight antenatal contacts during pregnancy, with the first visit before twelve weeks.
At visits the health worker checks your weight, blood pressure and blood level, and offers tests for HIV, malaria and other conditions. Go to every visit.

## When to see a midwife or doctor
Please see a midwife or doctor at a health facility if you cannot keep food or water down, feel very weak or dizzy, look very pale, or have swelling of the face or hands with a headache.
Do not wait if you have vaginal bleeding or severe belly pain. Go to a hospital immediately.
