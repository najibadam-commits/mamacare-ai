# Postnatal Recovery - First Six Weeks

## Postnatal check-ups
Every mother and baby should be checked by a health worker within twenty-four hours of birth. Further checks are recommended around day three, between day seven and day fourteen, and at six weeks.
Stay at the health facility for at least the first twenty-four hours after a normal delivery.

## Lochia: normal bleeding after birth
After birth the womb sheds blood and tissue. This vaginal discharge is called lochia and it changes over the weeks.
Lochia rubra is bright or dark red and lasts for the first few days. It may contain small clots.
Lochia serosa is pink or brown and watery. It usually follows from about day four to day ten.
Lochia alba is yellowish-white and creamy. It may last until about four to six weeks after birth.
Bleeding that slowly gets lighter is normal. Use clean pads and change them often. Do not use tampons or put anything in the vagina.

## Perineal and wound care
Wash your hands before and after using the toilet and before touching your stitches or your baby.
Rinse the perineum with clean water after passing urine or stool, and wipe or pat from front to back.
Change pads often and keep the area clean and dry. Sitting in clean warm water may ease soreness.
Eat fibre-rich foods and drink plenty of water to avoid constipation. Ask your health worker about pain relief rather than choosing medicines yourself.
If you had a caesarean section, keep the wound clean and dry and follow your surgeon's advice on lifting and activity.

## Rest and recovery
Rest whenever the baby sleeps. Accept help with cooking, cleaning and older children.
Eat nutritious meals and drink plenty of fluids, especially if you are breastfeeding.
Start gentle walking and pelvic floor exercises when your health worker says it is safe.
Talk to your health worker about family planning. Spacing pregnancies at least two years apart is healthier for mother and baby.

## Newborn basics
Place the baby skin-to-skin and breastfeed within the first hour. Give only breast milk for the first six months.
Keep the baby warm and keep the cord stump clean and dry. Do not put anything on the cord unless the health worker advises it.

## Emotional health
Many mothers feel tearful, anxious or moody in the first two weeks. This is often called the baby blues and usually passes.
Tell someone if sadness, worry or hopelessness lasts longer than two weeks, if you cannot sleep even when the baby sleeps, or if you cannot cope with daily care.
These can be signs of postnatal depression, which is common and treatable. A midwife or doctor can help.
If you have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, tell someone you trust and go to a health facility immediately.

## Postpartum danger signs
Go to a hospital immediately if you have heavy bleeding that soaks a pad in an hour, large clots, foul-smelling discharge, fever, severe belly pain, severe headache, blurred vision, fits, or a swollen painful leg.
Also go immediately if the baby is very hot or cold, feeds poorly, breathes fast, has fits, or looks yellow.
